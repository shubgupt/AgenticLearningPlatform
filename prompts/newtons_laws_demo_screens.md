Each scenario below follows the same 4-screen arc: **Hook → Sandbox → Concept → Diagnostic (with Mistake Genome branch)**.

## S9 — High School / Soccer / 3rd Law

**Screen 1 — Hook**
> "When a foot strikes a ball, two forces exist simultaneously — one on the ball, one on the foot. Are they equal?"
> `[Analyze the pair ⚽]`

**Screen 2 — Sandbox**
> "Toggle force vectors on both foot and ball during contact."
> `[Show Foot→Ball] [Show Ball→Foot] [Show Both] [Compare Magnitudes]`

**Screen 3 — Concept**
> "**Newton's Third Law:** For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction: F(foot→ball) = −F(ball→foot). The forces are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and act on *different* objects — which is why they don't cancel out."

**Screen 4 — Diagnostic**

>
> *If wrong →* "This points to the 'unequal force pair' misconception — a common one. The forces are always equal in magnitude regardless of mass; let's revisit why."

## S12 — High School / Barbie / 3rd Law

## Notes on Academic Invariance

Across all 18 scenarios, the physics stays identical: F_net = ma, force pairs are always equal-and-opposite, and constant velocity implies zero net force. Only the vocabulary, math depth, examples (soccer / Barbie / anime-stories), and interactivity scaffold change by grade band — the underlying laws are never simplified into something inaccurate.
